## **Envisioning the Future of Money**

When looking for new approaches to financial inclusion, the Interledger Foundation turned to artists: people whose minds see the world inventively and creatively. The Future Money Grant was co-designed with artist and innovator Kokayi Walker, and invited creators to challenge the assumptions built into financial systems and imagine alternatives, using any medium or artistic discipline.

## **What Grantees Got**

The grant offered $25,000 to the chosen artists and a platform to showcase their work. As a prompt, we asked that the proposed pieces consider future themes, such as decentralization, pan-border interconnectivity, financial services that work for people, and new cultural understandings of the fluidity of money.

## **Impact Stories**

Eight artists were selected from over 100 visionary submissions to explore how technology, equity, and creativity can converge to reimagine how we get paid, collaborate, and thrive. From a collage of lottery tickets to a hologram to an elaborate pencil drawing of a large waterworks, the artists created visions that challenge our understanding of the future of money.

## **Reflecting on the impact**

The Future Money Grant exemplified a bold, intentional effort to bridge the gap between artistic expression and financial inclusion. 

Eight artists and collectives were selected from over 100 international submissions. They worked across various media, including film, installation, food art, sculpture, performance, interactive media, and game design. Their projects addressed themes such as digital exclusion, village banking, speculative economics, communal wealth-sharing, and the cultural mythology of money.

The selected works were presented in an exhibition and featured in accompanying documentation, including public presentations and podcast episodes. 

Crucially, the Future Money Grant emphasized representation, co-creation, and inclusivity, challenging the traditional gatekeeping that often limits access to funding and visibility for BIPOC, LGBTQIA+, and marginalized artists. Grantees were invited into the design process, ensuring the resulting work was not only innovative but also rooted in community needs, accessible technologies, and systems of care.

By placing artists at the center of the conversation about finance, the Future Money Grant reframed money not just as a tool of the transaction but as a material of meaning, a system of values, and a space for radical reimagination.
