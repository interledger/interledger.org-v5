faqSections:
  - heading: 'About the Grant'
    items:
      - question: '1. What is the Digital Financial Services Grant?'
        answer: "The Digital Financial Services Grant is an Interledger Foundation funding program for financial services providers that are ready to implement interoperable payment infrastructure.\n\nThe grant will support organizations to integrate the Interledger technology stack (Rhyza, Interledger Protocol, Open Payments APIs), and test digital financial services with real users, customers, merchants, or institutional partners."
      - question: '2. How much funding is available?'
        answer: 'Selected organizations may receive up to $350,000 USD per award.'
      - question: '3. How many organizations will receive funding?'
        answer: 'The Interledger Foundation expects to make a maximum of **three awards.**'
      - question: '4. How long is the grant period?'
        answer: 'Projects will be implemented over **18 months**.'
      - question: '5. Are there geographic priorities for this funding opportunity?'
        answer: "Yes. While applications may be considered from eligible organizations in different markets, the Interledger Foundation is particularly interested in strengthening network participation in Colombia, the wider Americas, and across multiple markets or regions where new integrations could create broader interoperability and ecosystem value.\n\nProject quality, readiness, and strategic value will remain important considerations in the selection process."
  - heading: 'Eligibility'
    items:
      - question: '6. Who can apply?'
        answer: "The grant is intended for regulated or regulation-ready financial service providers.\n\nEligible organizations may include credit unions, savings and credit cooperatives, remittance providers, community-based financial institutions, digital wallet providers, and other regulated/regulation-ready financial service providers.\n\nApplicants should have the organizational and technical capacity to undertake integration and progress toward a real-world pilot during the grant period."
      - question: '7. Does my organization need to be licensed?'
        answer: 'Applicants should demonstrate either appropriate regulatory authorization or a credible path toward obtaining the authorization required for the proposed service. Organizations that are still progressing toward authorization should clearly explain their current status, outstanding requirements, expected timelines, and how regulatory approvals will affect implementation.'
      - question: '8. Can early-stage startups apply?'
        answer: "The program is intended primarily for implementation-ready financial service providers.\n\nAn early-stage organization would therefore need to demonstrate sufficient technical, regulatory, operational, financial, and organizational capacity to undertake the proposed integration and pilot successfully."
      - question: '9. Do we need an existing customer base?'
        answer: "Not necessarily. Applicants should either have an existing customer base or present a credible plan for onboarding users, customers, merchants, financial institutions, or other partners during implementation.\n\nProjects should be capable of progressing beyond a technical demonstration to testing in a real-world environment."
  - heading: 'Technical Requirements'
    items:
      - question: '10. Do applicants need previous experience with Interledger?'
        answer: "No. Previous Interledger implementation experience is not required.\n\nApplicants should, however, demonstrate sufficient technical readiness, internal expertise, or implementation support to successfully undertake Rhyza integration during the grant period."
      - question: '11. Is Rhyza integration required?'
        answer: "Yes. [Rhyza](/tech/rhyza/) integration is a core component of this funding opportunity. Applicants should explain how the technology fits within their proposed architecture, payment flow, and digital financial-service use case.\n\nRhyza is the next generation of Rafiki. It keeps Rafiki's purpose and is rebuilt around what its deployments taught us, with an architecture designed for larger networks and more connected apps. Rafiki was recognized as a Digital Public Good in 2026, and Rhyza carries the same commitment to open, freely available infrastructure.\n\nRhyza is free, open source software that connects a financial service provider’s existing infrastructure to Interledger, so it can make payments across systems and currencies and offer Open Payments-enabled accounts and services. It is the next generation of Rafiki, rebuilt for larger networks and easier integration.\n\nRhyza is currently in alpha and is expected to be production-ready by the end of Q2 2027. Grantees begin work at the start of Q2 2027, so early integration and testing will take place alongside the final stages of Rhyza’s development, with support from our technical team."
      - question: '12. Is Open Payments also part of this grant?'
        answer: "Yes. Rhyza implements Open Payments, so applicants should consider how Open Payments will support the proposed payment experience and how it will be incorporated into the project's technical architecture and implementation.\n\nLearn more about [Open Payments APIs](/tech/open-payments/)."
      - question: '13. Is this grant primarily for research or prototyping?'
        answer: "While development, testing, and validation may form part of the project, the grant is intended for implementation-ready organizations.\n\nCompetitive proposals should demonstrate a credible pathway from technical integration and interoperability testing to a pilot involving real users, customers, merchants, or institutional partners."
  - heading: 'Use of Funds'
    items:
      - question: '14. What activities can the grant funds be used for?'
        answer: "Funding may support costs associated with technical integration, infrastructure improvements, API development, security, regulatory and compliance readiness, network and settlement testing, customer or partner onboarding, pilot deployment, monitoring and evaluation, documentation, and other activities directly related to successful implementation. Proposed costs should be clearly connected to the project's implementation objectives."
      - question: '15. Can grant funding support regulatory or licensing work?'
        answer: 'Yes. Eligible activities may include legal support, licensing-related activities, risk-management frameworks, data protection, and security compliance where these activities are necessary for successful implementation.'
      - question: '16. Can applicants build on existing technology or infrastructure?'
        answer: 'Yes. Applicants may build upon existing platforms, infrastructure, products, customer relationships, and technical systems. Applications should clearly distinguish between what already exists and what will be developed, adapted, or integrated through the grant.'
  - heading: 'Application Process'
    items:
      - question: '17. How does the application process work?'
        answer: "The application process has two stages.\n\nDuring Phase 1, organizations submit a streamlined initial application describing their organization, proposed project, implementation approach, anticipated outcomes, capacity, budget, and indicative workplan. Applications will be reviewed for strategic fit, feasibility, readiness, and potential ecosystem value.\n\nShortlisted applicants will then submit a detailed full proposal."
      - question: '18. What will shortlisted applicants be asked to provide during Phase 2?'
        answer: 'Invited applicants should expect to provide additional information on technical architecture, implementation planning, milestones, regulatory readiness, budget, risk management, monitoring and evaluation, pilot design, and sustainability.'
      - question: '19. Does being invited to Phase 2 guarantee funding?'
        answer: 'No. An invitation to Phase 2 means that an application has advanced to the next phase of the competitive selection process. Final funding decisions will be made following review of the full proposals.'
  - heading: 'Selection'
    items:
      - question: '20. What will the Interledger Foundation consider when reviewing applications?'
        answer: "Applications will be assessed based on the overall strength and readiness of the proposed project, including its alignment with the goals of the grant, the relevance of the proposed use case, technical feasibility and integration readiness, and the applicant’s regulatory, compliance, operational, and organizational capacity. Reviewers will also consider the organization’s potential to participate meaningfully in an interoperable payments network, its ability to pilot the proposed solution with real users or partners, and the quality and practicality of its implementation approach.\_\n\nAdditional consideration will be given to the project’s potential to improve access, efficiency, or affordability in digital financial services, its prospects for sustainability beyond the grant period, and the value it could contribute to the wider Interledger ecosystem.\_\n\nGeographic representation and opportunities to strengthen or expand the network may also be considered when shaping the final cohort."
  - heading: 'During the Grant'
    items:
      - question: '21. What support will selected grantees receive?'
        answer: "Each organization will receive dedicated support from an Interledger Foundation Program Lead and Technical Lead.\n\nSupport will also include monthly implementation sessions, ongoing access to technical assistance, ecosystem connections, peer-learning opportunities, and opportunities to showcase project progress and results."
      - question: '22. What reporting will be required?'
        answer: "Awardees will be responsible for participating in regular project reporting and providing updates throughout the grant period. This may include progress reports, project communications, milestone updates, and final impact and learning documentation.\_\n\nReporting may cover areas such as technical implementation, regulatory and compliance progress, project milestones, network participation, budget performance, pilot results, adoption or usage data, lessons learned, and plans for sustainability beyond the grant period."
      - question: '23. How will grant funds be disbursed?'
        answer: "Grant funding will be released in milestone-based tranches aligned with project implementation timelines and agreed payment intervals.\_\n\nPayments will be made following satisfactory progress against agreed milestones."
      - question: '24. What does a successful project look like?'
        answer: "By the end of implementation, successful projects should aim to demonstrate:\n\n* Working Rhyza integration\n* A live Interledger connection with at least one partner institution\n* Live transactions involving real users\n* Evidence of improved financial-service delivery\n* Useful implementation evidence and documentation\n* A credible strategy for sustaining or scaling the service beyond the grant."
      - question: '25. Where can I get more information about the grant opportunity?'
        answer: "Applicants are encouraged to review the full application guidelines and supporting resources before applying.\n\n* **Grant Resource Kit:** Application guidelines, templates, FAQs, and supporting materials: [program resource kit](https://drive.google.com/drive/u/0/folders/1drmAFOrsQxVKOyKxGrKN6YXca2w6ppFt)\_\n* **Technical Resources:** Learn more about Rhyza, Open Payments APIs: [https://interledger.org/tech/dev-portal/](/tech/dev-portal/)\n* **Program Questions:** contact us at [financialservices@interledger.org](mailto:financialservices@interledger.org)."
