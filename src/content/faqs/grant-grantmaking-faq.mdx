  - heading: 'About the Interledger Foundation'
    items:
      - question: 'What is the Interledger Foundation?'
        answer: "The Interledger Foundation is a private operating foundation incorporated in California, United States. We steward open payment technologies, including the Interledger Protocol, the Open Payments API, and the Web Monetization technology standard, and make grants to advance access to digital financial services globally.\n\nWe are not a financial institution of any kind, we do not operate a payment network, nor do we earn revenue from payments. We are not a cryptocurrency, do not have a token, and our technology does not use blockchain. Our role is to support the development, adoption, and governance of open standards that enable interoperable, inclusive payment infrastructure."
      - question: 'What is the Interledger Foundation’s grantmaking mission?'
        answer: "We fund work that advances equitable access to digital financial services. We prioritize projects and individuals that help bring underserved populations into the benefits of open payment infrastructure and the digital economy. This includes people in low and middle income economies, migrants, refugees, and those historically excluded from traditional financial systems. We fund technical integration work, policy and advocacy, research, communities of practice, and the next generation of scholars and practitioners in this field.\n\nWe believe that the people closest to the problem are often best placed to design the solution. Our grantmaking tries to reflect that conviction."
      - question: 'What is the relationship between the Interledger Foundation’s protocol and standards stewardship work and its grantmaking?'
        answer: "The Interledger Foundation occupies a dual role: we develop and maintain open payment protocols and standards, and we fund their adoption and the broader ecosystem around them. Some of our grantmaking is directly connected to Interledger-related technologies, such as our Digital Financial Services technology integration grants. Other programs support the wider digital financial health field regardless of whether a grantee’s work involves Interledger specifically. These include our Public Policy Activation grantmaking, our Fellowship program, and our Call for Papers from scholars and independent researchers.\n\nWe also engage actively in governance and standards bodies, including the W3C, the OpenWallet Foundation, and the United Nations Internet Governance Forum."
      - question: 'How does the Interledger Foundation differ from other funders?'
        answer: "We have genuine admiration for the work of our peer funders, many of whom have built formidable programs and funded important work over many years. Where we differ is in our approach.\n\nWe are participatory rather than directive. We engage applicants and grantees as partners in a shared learning process and are intentionally experimental. We accept that some grants will not achieve their intended outcomes, and we treat that as information rather than failure. We are agile and we work to move at the speed our grantees need. Perhaps most distinctively, we deliberately seek out individuals and organizations that may not have access to funding elsewhere. Leaders who are early in their development, operating outside traditional philanthropic networks, or working in communities that larger funders have historically passed over are exactly the people we are trying to reach. Their proximity to real-world challenges, and their understanding of the communities they serve, is a form of expertise that we take seriously."
      - question: 'Is the Interledger Foundation a cryptocurrency initiative?'
        answer: 'No, and this is one of the most common misconceptions about our work. The Interledger Foundation is a nonprofit grantmaking foundation working to expand access to safe, responsible, and affordable financial services for communities who have traditionally been excluded from such offerings. We know the word ‘ledger’ has led some people to assume a connection to cryptocurrencies, however our name Interledger refers to inter-linking (accounting) ledgers. We do not issue or hold cryptocurrency, we do not operate a blockchain, and we do not have a token.'
      - question: 'What are Open Payments in an Interledger context?'
        answer: "Many of our grant programs refer to Open Payments, but we use the term in a way that varies slightly from how the financial services sector, particularly in Europe, understands the term.\n\nIn European financial regulation, open payments or open banking refers broadly to the requirement under frameworks such as PSD2 for banks to open their infrastructure to third-party providers via APIs. It describes a regulatory model that enables competition and consumer data portability. When the Interledger Foundation uses the term Open Payments, we are referring specifically to the Open Payments API: a technical standard we developed and steward that enables payment initiation and authorization across Interledger-connected accounts. It is a specific piece of technology, not a regulatory concept.\n\nThe two uses of the term are not in conflict, and the underlying goals are closely aligned. But they are not the same thing. If you work in European regulatory contexts and encounter the term Open Payments in our grant materials, please read it as a reference to our API standard rather than to the PSD2 framework or its successors. If you are uncertain whether your work fits one of our programs, we encourage you to contact us directly."

      - question: 'How did the Grant for the Web call for proposals in 2025 differ from the original 2019 program?'
        answer: "The original Grant for the Web was a broad funding initiative between Coil, Creative Commons, and Mozilla supporting experimentation with the Web Monetization proposed standard across a wide range of creators, developers, and publishers. It was exploratory by design: our predecessor, also named Grant for the Web, was seeding a new idea and learning what was possible.\n\nGrant for the Web in 2025 was more focused. The Interledger Foundation had a specific hypothesis: that there is a viable revenue model for digital content publishers that sits between advertising-funded free access and subscription paywalls. The journalism and publishing sector is under serious financial pressure. Advertising rates are declining, subscription fatigue is real, and dominant platforms limit who can participate and how. We believe Web Monetization, which enables readers to passively and automatically pay for content they value without going through an intermediary platform, could offer a genuine alternative.\n\nGrant for the Web in 2025 therefore targeted two specific audiences. The first was established digital content publishers with engaged audiences, particularly those that already use donations or advertising as their primary revenue model and are willing to test an advertising-free experience for users with active Web Monetization budgets. The second was publishing platforms that use open, standards-based protocols for content distribution and are already widely adopted."

    items:
      - question: 'What are the reporting requirements for Interledger Foundation grantees?'
        answer: "Reporting requirements are set out in each grant agreement and vary by program and grant size. Grantees are typically required to submit narrative progress reports at agreed intervals (commonly at the midpoint of a grant and at its conclusion), and financial reports detailing expenditure against the approved budget.\n\nWe may also invite grantees to participate in the Interledger Summit, in-person unconferences, virtual community calls, or podcast interviews.       - question: 'How does the Interledger Foundation approach grants that are not meeting their objectives?'
        answer: "We take a learning-oriented approach. If a grant is not meeting its objectives, we expect grantees to raise this proactively rather than waiting for a formal reporting deadline. We will work with grantees to understand the reasons, explore whether adjustments are warranted, and determine the best path forward together.\n\nWe do not treat difficulties as failures of character or competence. We accept that some grants will not achieve their intended outcomes, and we think the field learns more from honest accounts of what did not work than from cherrypicked narratives of success."
      - question: 'Does the Interledger Foundation allow adaptive management during grant implementation?'
        answer: 'Yes. Conditions change, and effective implementation sometimes requires adjustments to planned activities, timelines, or approaches. Grantees may request budget or activity modifications by submitting a Scope Change request to their program contact, explaining the rationale for the proposed change. Material modifications require written approval from us before they are implemented.'
      - question: 'Are no-cost extensions available?'
        answer: "Yes, no-cost extensions are available for most grants.\n\nA no-cost extension allows you to extend the period of your grant without additional funding, giving you more time to complete the proposed work.\n\nA few important points to be aware of:\n\n* Each grantee is entitled to one no-cost extension per grant, for a maximum of six months.\n* Extensions are not automatic: they must be requested and are subject to approval by your program officer.   - heading: 'Use of Artificial Intelligence'
    items:
      - question: 'Can I use AI tools when preparing my grant application or progress reports?'
        answer: "We recognize that AI tools are part of many people’s professional workflows, and we do not prohibit their use. But we want to be direct about how their use affects how we evaluate applications.\n\nWe value original thought, creative thinking, and detailed specificity. What makes an application competitive is not how it is written but what it contains: a clear and grounded idea, a credible plan, specific evidence of your skills and experience, and an honest account of what you intend to do and why. AI tools can help you structure and refine your thinking, but they cannot supply that substance. Applications where the writing is fluent but generic, and where the specificity and voice of the applicant are absent, will be viewed less favorably.\n\nWe are becoming more adept at identifying AI-generated content, and we have made it a priority to understand its prevalence in what we receive."
      - question: 'What kinds of AI use are acceptable, and what should I avoid?'
        answer: "Successful applications are specific. They describe your skills and past experience in concrete terms, explain why you are the right person or organization to do this work, and demonstrate that you understand both the problem and the context you are working in. AI cannot provide that specificity, but it might be able to help you with grammar checking and proofreading; restructuring or improving the clarity of text you have written; and translation assistance.\n\nUses of AI that will weaken your application:\n\n* Generating your budget: Your project budget is a core part of our assessment. Costs must be accurate, grounded in your specific context, and consistent with your proposed activities. AI cannot do this for you.\n* Using AI to describe Interledger technologies: Current AI models frequently misrepresent or conflate Interledger-related concepts. Inaccurate technical references will reduce the competitiveness of your application. Please consult our Developers Portal directly.\n* Relying on AI for fact-checking, data aggregation, or citations: Current models have well-documented limitations in these areas and can produce plausible-sounding but inaccurate results.\n* Using AI to characterize your alignment with our mission or programs: This is something only you can speak to authentically, and we will notice if it reads as though it was generated rather than considered."
      - question: 'Are there legal and ethical concerns about AI use I should be aware of?'
        answer: "Yes, and we take them seriously. A few issues we ask applicants to consider:\n\n* Many AI tools process your inputs and may use them to train future models. Be careful not to share confidential information, unpublished research, personal data, or proprietary organizational assets through AI tools without understanding the terms of service.\n* There are unresolved legal and ethical questions about whether AI models trained on copyrighted material without consent create liability for users. We encourage applicants to think carefully about whether their use of AI contributes to these harms.\n* AI systems rely on extensive data work, including collection, cleaning, annotation, and content moderation. This work is frequently outsourced to contractors in low and middle-income countries under precarious conditions, with low pay and significant mental health risks. These harms are well documented. The Interledger Foundation will not knowingly fund projects that benefit from exploitative data labor practices or non-consensual data harvesting.\n* AI carries a significant and growing environmental cost. Each query to a large language model produces measurable carbon emissions, and the energy demands of data centers have increased sharply in recent years. We encourage applicants to use AI tools proportionately and to be aware of their environmental footprint."
      - question: 'Do I have to disclose if I have used AI in preparing my application?'
        answer: "Yes. If you use AI tools in preparing your application, you are required to disclose this on the application form. You should indicate which categories of use apply (for example, idea generation, grammar editing, literature review, translation, or graphics) and which tools you used. We may request your prompts.\n\nThis requirement applies to grant applications, progress reports, blog posts, and other programmatic outputs submitted to the Interledger Foundation. Specific programs may have additional requirements, which will be detailed in the relevant program’s application guidelines."

      - question: 'Can the Interledger Foundation fund for-profit organizations?'
        answer: 'Yes, and with care. As a private operating foundation, we are subject to specific legal requirements when granting to for-profit entities, including expenditure responsibility obligations that require us to ensure funds are used for charitable purposes. We recognize that much important work in open payment infrastructure is being done by mission-aligned companies and social enterprises. However, we do apply additional scrutiny to ensure that funded activities serve the broader ecosystem rather than private shareholders.'
      - question: 'What is the Interledger Foundation’s approach to intellectual property produced through grant-funded work?'
        answer: "The Interledger Foundation encourages and prefers the use of open licenses for outputs produced through grant-funded work, as openness supports broad access, reuse, and ecosystem growth.\n\nFor software and code, we generally recommend the Apache License 2.0 or an equivalent permissive open source license. For other outputs, such as research, we typically suggest Creative Commons licenses like CC BY or CC BY-SA to ensure work is accessible and attributable.\n\nAt the same time, we recognize that contexts vary and that a one-size-fits-all approach to licensing is not always appropriate. We are open to discussing alternative licensing models where they better support the sustainability, impact, or local relevance of a project. In some cases, this may include more graduated approaches to openness, or models that enable grantees to build viable, self-sustaining pathways for their work.\n\nWe encourage applicants to raise any proposed deviations from default open licensing during the proposal stage so that we can explore options together. While we do not claim ownership of intellectual property created by grantees, we aim to ensure that outputs remain meaningfully accessible to the communities they are intended to serve, in ways that are appropriate to their context."
      - question: 'Are there restrictions on the countries that the Interledger Foundation can fund?'
        answer: 'Yes. As a private operating foundation incorporated in the United States, the Interledger Foundation is legally required to comply with US sanctions law. This means we cannot make grants to individuals or organizations located in, or controlled by, countries or parties subject to sanctions administered by the US Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC). We conduct sanctions screening as part of our standard due diligence process for all applicants. If you are uncertain whether your country or organization is affected, we encourage you to contact OFAC before investing time in an application.'
      - question: 'What does the contracting process entail?'
        answer: "We will prepare a written grant agreement that outlines each party’s responsibilities. Once it has been reviewed internally, we will share a draft with the grantee for their own review asking that questions or concerns be flagged before the agreement is finalized. Once all parties are happy with the text, we will circulate the grant agreement for electronic signature via the DropboxSign platform. You will be provided a countersigned copy of the agreement.\n\nGrants are not approved until the grant agreement is signed by all signatories. No funds can be transferred and no program costs incurred prior to the signing of the grant agreement."
      - question: 'How and when are grant funds paid?'
        answer: "We generally pay in installments. A portion of the grant is typically disbursed within 30 days of the grant agreement being signed, with the remaining funds released following submission and acceptance of the interim report.\n\nThe exact payment schedule varies by program and grant size, and we are open to discussing arrangements that work for the grantee’s context. If a different structure would better support your ability to deliver the work, raise it with us before the grant agreement is finalized."
