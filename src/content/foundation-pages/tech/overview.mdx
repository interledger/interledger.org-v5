<Paragraph>

Interledger technology lets banks, wallets, and financial institutions connect through shared standards, open protocols, and open-source tools built for real-world integration.

Today’s payment systems run on separate networks, different rules, and disconnected infrastructure. Each new connection means custom integrations, separate agreements, and ongoing coordination, rebuilt from scratch every time. Interledger replaces that with one open protocol and a shared set of standards, so systems can work together without rebuilding integrations every time.

</Paragraph>

<Paragraph>

## The Interledger Stack

Interledger is a stack of open protocols that work in layers, modeled on the architecture of the internet. Each layer has one delegated responsibility and knows how to communicate with the layers directly above and below it. That structure is what makes the technology adaptable, because a layer can be improved or replaced without disrupting the others.

</Paragraph>

<SplitLayout imagePosition="left" displayRatio="1:1" imageSrc="/uploads/img/original/Diagram_2_26bebf3ebf.jpg" imageAlt="Diagram of the Interledger protocol stack. From top to bottom: SPSP and Open Payments at the application layer; STREAM at the transport layer; ILP at the protocol layer; ILP over HTTP at the link layer; and fiat, crypto, or mobile money at the settlement or physical layer.">
### **Application Protocols (Open Payments and Simple Payments Setup Protocol)**

Define how an application arranges a payment with an account: requesting it, quoting the amount and any fees, and getting the payer’s approval.

[Explore Open Payments](/tech/open-payments)

### **Transport Protocols (STREAM)**

Breaks a payment into packets, encrypts and sends them across ILP, and reassembles them reliably at the other end.

[Learn more about STREAM](/developers/rfcs/stream-protocol)

### **The Interledger Protocol (ILP)**

Routes value across those systems. It works out where a payment needs to go and passes it on, without needing to know what sits underneath.

[Learn more about ILP](/tech/interledger-protocol)

### **Link Layer Protocols**

Provide two-way authenticated communication between account holders or their financial service providers.

[Explore link layer protocols](/developers/rfcs/interledger-architecture/#link-protocols)

### **Ledgers and Payment Systems**

The base of the stack: the banks, wallets, and mobile money services that hold accounts and move funds. Settlement happens outside of Interledger, allowing for increased competition and no vendor lock-in.

[Explore ledger protocols](/developers/rfcs/interledger-architecture/#ledger-protocols)

</SplitLayout>

<CardGrid title="Why it is Built This Way" ariaLabel="Design decisions behind the Interledger Stack" variant="Info" columns="Two">
<InfoCard heading="Delegated Responsibility">

Each layer does one job and relies on the others to do theirs, which keeps every part simple.

</InfoCard>
<InfoCard heading="Clean Interfaces">

A layer only needs to understand the concepts of the layers directly above and below it, not the whole system.

</InfoCard>
<InfoCard heading="Interchangeability">

Layers can be swapped without disrupting the rest. A new ledger technology at the base, or a new application at the top, works without changing the protocol in the middle.

</InfoCard>
<InfoCard heading="Modularity and Scale">

Because the pieces are independent, the stack can grow and adapt over time while the core stays stable.

</InfoCard>
</CardGrid>

You can build on Interledger on your own, using the open documentation, repositories, test tools, and with support from the community. You can also build with support: the Interledger Foundation funds teams and institutions integrating the Interledger stack or implementing Open Payments through its grant programs.
