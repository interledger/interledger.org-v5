## What you Will Find Here

Good policy on payments depends on evidence that does not yet exist in one place. This library is our attempt to gather it.

Some of the work here is independent scholarship, funded by the Interledger Foundation but shaped entirely by its authors, who chose their own questions and published what they found. Some were commissioned to answer a question we needed answered. Some is analysis, written with or by partners and advocates. And some is neither research nor analysis in the conventional sense, but an attempt to make a system that resists explanation legible in a more creative form.

The collection covers regulation and supervisory practice, protocol and infrastructure design, the barriers that keep people at the edges of the financial system, and what happens when open payment standards meet the realities of a particular country, currency, and set of rules.

<Faq heading="Independent Research">

<FaqItem question="By Design? How Design Harms and Confuses Consumers in Digital, Finance Products">

- **Abstract:** An interactive report on harmful design patterns, sometimes called dark patterns, in banking and financial services, a domain the existing literature has largely passed over in favor of e-commerce and privacy. Over eight months the author combined a literature review and systematic mapping of harmful design taxonomies with a design analysis of specific platforms, including Chime and SoLo Funds, and user research with four Chilean immigrants in London observed making live international transfers. Interviews with current and former US and UK regulators and with design and consumer protection experts supplement the user work. The report distinguishes harmful design from merely unfriendly design and from the necessary friction of identity verification, and it proposes a new category of harmful design specific to financial services: the failure of neobanks and payment platforms to disclose prominently that they are not banks and what protections consumers therefore lose. It closes with practical recourse for consumers and an argument that financial literacy cannot substitute for regulation.
- **Author(s):** Caroline Sinders (Convocation Research+Design)
- **Publication date:** 2025

</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="Banking on Rights: A Case Study of Digital Financial Inclusion for Blind Persons in Pakistan">

- **Abstract:** This paper examines digital financial inclusion for blind and low-vision banking customers in Pakistan, tracing the evolution of the State Bank of Pakistan's regulatory framework from 2009 to 2024. Drawing on documentary analysis, a survey of 120 respondents, and 18 semi-structured interviews, the study argues that financial exclusion has been transformed rather than eliminated: legacy barriers such as reliance on witnesses and institutional paternalism persist alongside emergent digital barriers, including inaccessible mobile applications, authentication systems, and ATMs. Informed by the capability approach, socio-technical systems theory, and the concept of institutional paternalism, the paper introduces "functional internet access" as a measure of substantive financial capability and proposes a six-pillar reform agenda centered on binding digital accessibility obligations, alternative authentication pathways, and disability-disaggregated supervisory monitoring.
- **Author(s):** Dr. Muhammad Shabbir
- **Publication date:** 2026

</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="The Missing Link: Remittances &amp; Socially Trusted Financial Intermediation as Key Element for Addressing Root Causes of Migration">

- **Abstract:** This paper argues that properly intermediated remittances represent a critical yet underutilized resource for addressing the root causes of migration. Current public policies have largely overlooked the potential of community-based financial institutions, when combined with Fintech infrastructure, to channel remittances into sustainable local development in rural, remittance-dependent regions.
- **Author(s):** Raúl Hinojosa-Ojeda, Roberto Valdovinos Alba, Isabel Cruz Hernández, Yvonne Su
- **Publication date:** September 2025

</FaqItem>

<FaqItem question="A Cashless Country &amp; The Future of Banks">

- **Abstract:** The financial world is rapidly shifting, with consumers increasingly questioning the value of cash and the definition of a traditional bank. This report is based on a national survey of 1,004 U.S. consumers, revealing the deep-rooted habits and changing views driving the industry's evolution.
- **Author(s):** This study was commissioned by the Interledger Foundation and conducted by third-party research firm Dynata.
- **Publication date:** October 24, 2025

</FaqItem>
