## We started with a question. We’re still working on the answer.

Interledger is designed to help systems operate as a network, and fund those working to make financial access open and fair at a global scale.

</SplitLayout>

<Paragraph>

## From Protocol to Global Movement

In 2015, Interledger started with a simple idea: money should move as easily as information.

That idea led Stefan Thomas and Evan Schwartz to create the Interledger Protocol: an open standard designed to route value across financial systems, the way the internet routes data across different networks.

Early experiments showed what that could mean in practice: from micropayments for artists and new revenue streams for publishers, to zombie-themed coding tutorials that brought open payments to a new generation of developers. In 2017, a test with the Bank of England proved that the Interledger Protocol could reduce the settlement time for large cross-border payments from four days to a matter of seconds.

But while the technology proved itself, a deeper problem emerged. Financial infrastructure had been built for institutions, not for the people and communities who depend on it. It was fragmented, expensive, or in some cases, simply unavailable.

In 2020, the Interledger Foundation was created to challenge that. It was structured as an independently governed foundation so the work could never be commercially captured or steered by any single interest.

</Paragraph>

<Paragraph>

We’ve taken the Interledger Protocol and built on it. We’ve developed the Open Payments API to connect financial systems, created Rafiki to turn the protocol from concept into a practical implementation, and engaged the policymakers and institutions who shape the environment for open payments to thrive.

Today, we are established as a credible voice in international payments policy, with a global community of developers, thought leaders, and advocates. We also fund those working to make financial access open and fair at a global scale, from connecting 140 credit unions in Mexico to supporting rural finance systems in Uganda.

Since 2020, we’ve allocated over $21M in grants across 300 projects in 46 countries, and skilled over a thousand students across four continents. Because we know that technology alone doesn't create change, people do.

For us, the system isn't fixed until it works for everyone. So we'll keep building the tech, growing the movement and shifting the system to make it happen.

</Paragraph>
