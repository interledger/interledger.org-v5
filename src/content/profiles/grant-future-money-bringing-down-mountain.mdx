## Bringing Down the Mountain

[Watch the video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X_O_cu6xlEQ&t=1s)
Listen to the full podcast here: [Bringing Down A
Mountain](https://podcast.interledger.org/@futuremoneypodcast/episodes/bringing-down-a-mountain)

Bringing Down a Mountain is a compelling documentary by filmmaker and civil society leader Subhashish Panigrahi that examines the significant disconnect between technological advancement and financial inclusion in rural and marginalized communities in India.

The film examines the paradox of digital growth in India, where nearly 46% of the world's digital transactions occur, yet 66% of the population lives in villages with limited or no internet access. At the heart of the story is a critical question: Does digital inclusion truly translate into financial empowerment?

Shot across diverse rural landscapes, the film examines how India's push toward a cashless economy, spearheaded by platforms such as the Unified Payments Interface (UPI), has left many behind. These systems, while hailed as revolutionary, often fail to reach those without digital infrastructure, financial literacy, or trust in state-backed financial tools. Bringing Down a Mountain challenges the myth of seamless inclusion. Instead, it reveals the lived realities of digital disparity, particularly among Indigenous groups and those historically excluded from formal financial systems.

Panigrahi, whose background includes digital rights advocacy and language preservation, brings a deeply personal and informed perspective to the work. His filmmaking journey began with grassroots documentation efforts, supported by organizations such as the Wikimedia Foundation and National Geographic. These early projects helped communities reclaim endangered languages and cultural practices, laying the foundation for his broader exploration of systemic exclusion through documentary film.

More than just a film, Bringing Down a Mountain is a call to rethink what real access means. It proposes that the path to true financial equity must be informed by local realities, cultural context, and the voices of those most impacted. By weaving together storytelling, advocacy, and research, the film serves as both a visual record and a catalyst for dialogue and policy change in financial justice.
