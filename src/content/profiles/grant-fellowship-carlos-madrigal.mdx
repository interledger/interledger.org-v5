**Fellowship Project**

Every organization needs to open an account, receive funds and make payments in order to operate. For many social-purpose organizations in Central America, each of those steps comes with requirements, costs and delays that are rarely documented.

Over 12 months, Carlos will map the payment routes these organizations use and document where those routes stall. He will also measure what those delays cost in time, money and risk. He will look at how banks, regulators, cooperatives, fintechs and funders see the same process from their side. The project combines interviews in several countries with comparative regulatory analysis. It will test where open payment standards could reduce friction and where the answer lies in regulation or provider incentives. Outputs will include a public report, a map of payment frictions and recommendations for the Interledger community, funders and financial-sector actors.

## **What Inspired His Work**

“A decade inside banks taught me how financial institutions assess risk, decide which clients to serve and work within their own constraints. Leading finance teams in the third sector later showed me the same decisions from the client’s side of the counter.”

Carlos kept meeting the same questions, one organization at a time, and wanted to understand the structure behind them. Throughout his career he has trained and mentored colleagues at every level, convinced that expertise only matters when it is shared.

## **Vision for the Future**

“I hope to bring regional evidence into \[the open payments] conversation and to connect the ecosystem with a part of the financial landscape it rarely sees up close.”

Carlos values open, interoperable standards because they work the same way for every provider, which makes the rules of access visible and comparable. He was drawn to the Interledger Foundation because it treats access as a question of infrastructure and rules as well as technology. Through his fellowship, he aims to move from solving individual cases to producing evidence that organizations, financial institutions and regulators can use.
