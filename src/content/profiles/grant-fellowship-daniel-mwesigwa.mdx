**Fellowship Project**

Daniel’s project, the Credit Infrastructure Observatory, maps the emerging Buy Now, Pay Later (BNPL) landscape in Kenya and the United States. In cities such as Nairobi and New York, AI-driven credit scoring uses alternative data about consumer behavior to offer credit through products like BNPL. For people shut out of traditional finance, BNPL has opened a way to take part in the economy. For people already carrying heavy debt, it has become another way to cover day-to-day needs. Concerns about algorithmic opacity and overindebtedness work against technology’s promise to expand financial inclusion.

Daniel will gather consumers’ own reflections on BNPL to understand how digital lending is changing and what policy and regulation these ecosystems need. Little reference material exists on this topic, and his research will produce independent evidence to fill that gap. It will also build consumer AI literacy and show where open infrastructure could play a role in BNPL ecosystems.

## **What Inspired His Work**

“I have always been inspired by the agencies exercised by users to localize and reshape their behaviors, routines, and practices around digital credit products.”

Daniel challenges the idea that “users” are passive, a view that mainstream technology actors often hold. His work sits where user agency meets a fast-changing fintech landscape. He studies people’s experiences alongside the technology itself to inform the public and influence policy.

## **Vision for the Future**

“The findings will inform community and policy action essential for championing responsible financial inclusion.”

Daniel is drawn to the community-centered values at the core of Interledger: fairness, equity and agency. He sees the rise of BNPL as both a technical and a cultural achievement, yet little is known about the infrastructure that makes it work across markets. By making that infrastructure visible, his research will show where power sits among the stakeholders in BNPL ecosystems. It will also give communities and policymakers the evidence they need to act.
