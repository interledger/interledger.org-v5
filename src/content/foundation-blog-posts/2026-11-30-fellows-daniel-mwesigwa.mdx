## **Mapping BNPL in Kenya and the US**

The global rise of BNPL has captured public attention and the imaginations of financial inclusion experts. BNPL is a new form of credit that enables consumers to pay for products and services in installments usually across a short period of time. Because BNPL platforms leverage big data and AI, they have dramatically expanded access to credit for people without formal credit histories, across geographies and income groups. In the US, BNPL has grown by over 20% annually since 2021, reaching an estimated $70 billion in 2025, while in Kenya, it has grown at 28% per year and is projected to nearly triple to $3.7 billion by 2031. BNPL has not only enabled economic participation for those financially excluded by traditional finance but also those overburdened by credit, seeking alternative options to meet their day-to-day needs, including the acquisition of smartphones, motorcycles, clothes, and even food and groceries.

While BNPL is increasingly shaping access to credit through data and AI-driven scoring, the infrastructures underpinning BNPL remain largely invisible to the low-income communities most affected and to experts who might otherwise intervene. Indeed, this opacity – including across technical and legal infrastructures – is reproducing concerns around overindebtedness and economic injustice; in part, undermining the very promises of technology to supercharge financial inclusion. What can a public record of the lived experiences of consumers tell us about the evolution of BNPL in Kenya and the US? And what policies and regulations might these worlds require?

## Your background

My motivation to pursue this work and to advance digital financial access comes from one question I have asked myself and others for over 10 years: Cui bono? Who benefits? (And if you may add: at whose expense?). 

My work since the mid 2010s has gravitated around understanding the intricacies of technology in order to inform the public and influence policy. Drawing on research and professional experience in the private sector, academia, and civil society, I have produced reports and policy briefs on the promises and pitfalls of technology for social good and democratic engagement in Africa. This work has informed policy across several countries in Eastern and Southern Africa as well as in international fora. 

Overall, I draw on sustained engagement with AI and fintech to understand the design, use, and impacts of novel digital lending models such as BNPL, contextualizing these models to the places they circulate while pursuing their implications with rigor and care. 

## **What you will do during the Fellowship**

My project’s main goal is to develop an Observatory that will bring into focus the infrastructure underlying BNPL as well as specific customer experiences with BNPL products, helping equip low-income communities and public interest technology actors with the right evidence, literacies, and tools to support stronger and meaningful financial participation.

Over 12 months, I will develop a web-based Credit Infrastructure Observatory, which will track key developments around BNPL in Kenya and the US, using a novel social messaging-based diary to document customer experiences with BNPL platforms alongside a policy audit to map and analyze the technical and regulatory regimes underlying those platforms. The digital diary method will record the experiences of borrowers from low- to moderate-income communities in Nairobi, Kenya and New York City, United States. The digital diary method will combine weekly text prompts with monthly voice check-ins. In conjunction with the digital diary, I will conduct data and policy audits, seeking to understand the technical, policy, and regulatory frameworks for BNPL in Kenya and the US.

To cultivate AI literacy amongst borrowers, I will produce short explainer blogs around BNPL, synthesizing materials from the data and policy audits and findings from anonymized borrower experience data. Additionally, I will co-produce a BNPL-themed card game with trilingual support for English, Kiswahili, and Spanish. These cards will be printed in local communities in Nairobi and NYC, deepening AI literacy, especially in offline, real-world settings.

While most of my work will be published in the community forum, some of it will appear in global public media channels.

## **An open invitation**

This project – and indeed my journey – would not be possible without the generosity of strangers and friends. My work is shaped as much by attention to the technical as to the social and political. If you develop APIs or know a thing or two about them, if you build BNPL credit scoring models, if you pore over the fine print that makes regulation (im)possible, I want to learn from you! While most of my work will be developed and managed across Nairobi and New York, the rapid growth of BNPL is a global phenomenon, and with it requires global (and local!) investigation, in part expanding literacy and possibilities for technical and policy intervention. This is an open invitation; I want to hear from you. Please get in touch via email, [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/mwsig/), or Slack!
